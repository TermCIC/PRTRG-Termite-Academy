01 — About

What the Academy is for

The Termite Academy is a training workshop for students, young scholars, early-career termite researchers, and industry professionals across the Pacific Rim. It exists to train the next generation of termite researchers and practitioners — not to be a conference.

Chiang Mai in early May sits in the right place and the right season for this: Northern Thailand carries high termite diversity, and the timing coincides with the dispersal flight season, so the light-trap night is a genuine field opportunity rather than a demonstration.

The programme is built around three things you will actually do:

Component 01Indoor lecturesTermite biology, ecology, evolution, taxonomy, and management principles, at CMU main campus
Component 02Field survey trainingA full day in the forest at the Mushroom Research Center, then light-trapping and night observation
Component 03Management practiceInspection, monitoring, and urban termite damage seen in the city with a live control demonstration

Who is here. 40 participants: 25 students, and 15 non-students — the invited lecturers, pest management professionals, and academics attending on their own account. Four organising helpers make 44 people on site. The cap is set by the teaching room.

Where you sleep. Students are resident at the Mae Hia station for all three nights, and their rooms are booked for them as a block — there is nothing to arrange. Non-students choose: ask the organisers to book a room, or book your own and travel in. Day 2 begins with an early minibus from Mae Hia to the Mushroom Research Center in Pa Pae.

04 — Places

One base, four working places

You live at Mae Hia. Lectures are at CMU main campus, fieldwork is at the Mushroom Research Center, and Day 3 takes you into the city.

Your base · all three nights

Mae Hia station

The Faculty of Agriculture’s Agricultural Innovation Research, Integration, Demonstration and Training Centre, at 155 Soi Mu Ban Nai Fan 2, Mae Hia, Mueang Chiang Mai. This is the accommodation and programme base.

The grounds carry farmland, grassland, scrub, ponds and woodland. That range of habitat in one accessible place is exactly what makes it a good teaching site: more termite microhabitats than a single transect, with toilets and shade nearby.

Mains power reaches the light-trap site, so the trap runs without a generator.

Day 2 · field survey and light trap

Mushroom Research Center

A private forest research venue in Pa Pae, Mae Taeng, used for daytime collecting and sampling, laboratory work, and the evening light trap.

Research use of samples or data from this site is governed by a written site and publication agreement. Authorship is based on documented scholarly contributions and the policy of the journal receiving the work.

Days 1 and 3 · lectures

CMU main campus

About 5 km from Mae Hia, roughly fifteen minutes by minibus. The opening ceremony, all six lectures, and the microscope practical happen here, with support from the Department of Entomology and Plant Pathology, Chiang Mai University.

Day 3 afternoon · demonstration

Chiang Mai city

Urban termite damage seen where it actually occurs, followed by a house termite control demonstration by a Chiang Mai pest management training partner (to be confirmed): inspection, control decision-making, a baiting system, and pesticide injection.

This is a working chemical site, not a classroom. You observe from a designated distance, wear the PPE issued to you, and follow the technician’s instructions without exception. Whether pesticide is injected live or only demonstrated depends on permission and safety conditions on the day.

06 — Safety

Rules that are not optional

Fieldwork, night work, and a chemical demonstration in three days. Read this before Day 2.

Daytime fieldwork

  • Stay with your assigned group and mentor
  • Stay inside the survey zone defined for your group — the station is working farmland with vehicles and machinery
  • Drink water before you feel thirsty; May is hot
  • Report any sting, bite, cut, or heat symptom to your mentor immediately
  • Sample without damaging planting, structures, or pond margins

Night work

  • Carry a working headlamp and a charged phone
  • Never leave the lit working area alone or unannounced
  • Watch footing around cables, the trap structure, and uneven ground
  • Know your group supervisor and the emergency contact for the night
  • Return to the accommodation as a group

Chemical demonstration · Day 3

  • The morning briefing is mandatory — no briefing, no site visit
  • Wear the PPE issued to you for the whole demonstration
  • Keep to the marked observation distance; do not approach injection or bait points
  • Do not handle chemicals, equipment, or bait stations yourself
  • No eating, drinking, or smoking on site
  • Follow the technician’s instructions immediately and without discussion

Insurance. Group accident cover for the three days is included in your registration fee. It does not replace your own travel and medical insurance, which you should arrange yourself. The signed field safety form and an emergency contact are required at registration on Day 1 — you cannot join Day 2 without them.
