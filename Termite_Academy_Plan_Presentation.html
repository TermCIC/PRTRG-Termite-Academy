01 — What it is02

What the Academy is

Three days of training for termite researchers and industry professionals in the Pacific Rim. Held in Chiang Mai, in the dispersal flight season.

Indoor lectures

Termite biology, ecology, evolution, taxonomy, and the principles of management. Delivered at CMU main campus.

Field survey training

A full day sampling forest habitat at the Mushroom Research Center, then a light trap and night observation of dispersal flights.

Applied management

Inspection and monitoring, then into the city to see urban termite damage and a live house control demonstration.

Purpose stated in the original PRTRG proposal

10 — Proposal11

The three control methods

Ask the Northern Pest Management Association (NPMA) to provide the site and the instructors for Day 3.

1 · Wood injection

Remedial treatment

Locating galleries in infested timber, drilling, injecting, and sealing. The technique for damage already inside a structure.

2 · Soil or tube injection

Barrier treatment

Rodding and trenching to build a treated zone, or pumping through a pre-installed reticulation system. The technique for keeping termites out.

3 · Baiting

Colony elimination

Installing and reading monitoring stations, and switching a monitor to toxicant bait. The technique that works on the colony rather than the building.

The three together are the whole strategic map of termite management — remedial, barrier, and colony elimination. A trainee who has handled all three has seen the real choices a practitioner makes, not a list of them.
Companies provide the site, the equipment, and the instructorIn exchange for being named partners of the Academy
11 — Proposal12

How Day 3 runs

Company premises in the morning, a real infested site at midday, back to the company to debrief.

1 · At the company

Morning

The three procedures demonstrated with their own equipment, in demonstration areas built for it. Safe, controlled, and close enough to watch properly. Groups rotate through the stations.

2 · At a real job site

Midday, together

A building with genuine termite damage, visited as one group with the technicians. Inspection in the conditions a practitioner actually meets — the one thing that cannot be staged.

3 · Back at the company

Afternoon

Debrief while it is fresh. What was chosen at that building and why, what the alternatives were, and the questions that only occur after seeing the real thing.

Why this order. Seeing the real site first would mean watching techniques you do not yet recognise. Seeing only the demonstration areas would leave the impression that termite control happens in tidy conditions. The return leg is what turns a site visit into teaching.
One coordinated movement with the company’s staff, not 40 people finding their own way between housesNPMA: request sent 15 November 2026, quotation pending

18 — Not included19

Not in the budget

Lecturer airfares are not in the figures above. Six lecturers at roughly THB 12,000–20,000 each is THB 72,000–120,000 — on its own larger than transport, accommodation and food combined. Adding it would push the total to about THB 212,000–281,000 and the fee needed to about THB 8,500 per person, which is beyond what students should be asked to pay.

If airfares are to be covered

  • They must come from outside the registration fee — sponsorship, PRTRG funds, CMU support, or a grant
  • Many lecturers can claim travel from their own institution or grants; ask before assuming the Academy must pay
  • Lecturer travel is a classic thing sponsors are willing to fund, and can be named as such

Also not covered

  • Trainee flights — every participant books their own
  • Lecturer accommodation — they choose their own hotels
  • Hotel nights before or after the Academy
  • Travel and medical insurance, and visa fees
Lecturers have all offered to teach without honorarium
